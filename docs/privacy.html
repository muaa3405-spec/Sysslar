Privacy policy

Last updated 25 November 2026

Sysslar is a desktop application for Windows and macOS, published as free and open-source software. This policy explains what the app stores, what it sends and to whom.

The short version: Sysslar has no servers and no accounts. Your tasks, notes, time entries and attachments are stored on your own computer. The developer of Sysslar receives no data about you or your use of the app — there is nowhere for it to be sent.

What Sysslar stores, and where

Everything you enter — tasks, notes, groups, reminders, attachments, tracked time, reports and settings — is stored in a database file in your user account on your own computer, together with automatic daily backups. None of it is transmitted anywhere unless you turn on one of the optional features described below.

What leaves your computer

Nothing, by default

With no optional feature enabled, Sysslar makes no network connections at all. There is no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no third-party tracking of any kind in the app.

Update check (optional)

If you enable the update check, Sysslar asks GitHub’s public release API whether a newer version exists. That request identifies the app and its version (for example Sysslar/1.5.2) and, like any web request, reaches GitHub with your IP address. It contains none of your tasks, notes or other content. GitHub’s handling of that request is covered by GitHub’s own privacy statement. You can leave the update check off and download new versions yourself.

Sync between your computers (optional)

If you turn on sync, Sysslar encrypts your data with AES-256 before it leaves your computer and stores the encrypted files in storage that you choose and control: a Google Drive, iCloud Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox folder, any shared or network folder, or — if you connect it — your own Google Drive account directly.

The encryption key is generated on your computer and stored only on the computers where you turn sync on, protected by your operating system’s key store (Windows DPAPI, macOS Keychain). The key is never sent to the developer, to the storage provider, or to anyone else. This means that whoever stores the files — Google, Apple, Microsoft, Dropbox or your own server — holds only encrypted data they cannot read.

Your storage provider will apply its own privacy policy to the files it stores, and will know the usual facts about the account storing them (file sizes, timestamps, and so on). Sysslar does not send your data anywhere else.

Connecting Google Drive

Connecting a Google account is optional and exists only as one of several places Sysslar can keep its encrypted sync files.

  • What Sysslar asks for. The drive.file permission, which grants access only to files the app itself creates. Sysslar cannot see, read or modify any other file in your Google Drive, and does not ask for permission to.
  • What it stores there. Only its own encrypted sync files, inside a folder it creates for the purpose. Their contents are unreadable without your encryption key.
  • Where the sign-in is kept. The access and refresh tokens Google issues are stored on your computer in the operating system key store, the same place as your encryption key. They are not transmitted to the developer.
  • What is read about you. Sysslar does not request your name, email address, contacts, profile or any other Google account information, and does not store any.
  • Disconnecting. You can turn sync off in Sysslar at any time, and you can revoke Sysslar’s access to your Google account at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Revoking access stops syncing; the files already in your Drive remain yours to keep or delete.

Sysslar’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data obtained through Google APIs is used solely to provide the sync feature you asked for; it is never sold, never transferred to anyone else, never used for advertising, and never read by humans.

What the developer receives

Nothing. Sysslar operates no servers, no accounts and no backend of any kind. If you choose to report a problem on GitHub, you decide what to include in that report, and it is public.

Children

Sysslar is a general-purpose task manager with no accounts and no data collection. It is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published on this page with a new date above. Material changes affecting how the app handles data will also be noted in the release notes of the version that introduces them.
